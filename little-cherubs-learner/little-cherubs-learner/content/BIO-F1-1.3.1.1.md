## 1. Lesson notes

**Competence ID:** BIO-F1-1.3.1.1 **Competence:** Classify types of food nutrients **Form:** 1 **Expected standard:** Types of food nutrients classified correctly

### Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. Name the seven classes of food nutrients.
2. Give Zambian food sources for each class.
3. Classify a food by its main nutrient.
4. Explain why the body needs each class of nutrient.

### What is a nutrient?

A **nutrient** is a substance in food that the body uses to stay alive, grow and stay healthy.

Most foods contain more than one nutrient. For example, kapenta has protein, fat and minerals. We **classify** a food by its **main nutrient**: the one it contains the most of, or is best known for.

### The seven classes of food nutrients

There are seven classes of food nutrients.

| Class | Zambian food sources | Main job in the body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbohydrates | Nshima, cassava, sweet potatoes, rice, bread, sugar cane | Give energy |
| Proteins | Kapenta, beans, groundnuts, eggs, chicken, beef, edible caterpillars, milk | Growth and repair of tissues |
| Lipids (fats and oils) | Cooking oil, butter, groundnuts, avocado, fatty meat | Store energy, keep the body warm, protect organs |
| Vitamins | Mangoes, oranges, guavas, pumpkin leaves (chibwabwa), rape, carrots | Keep the body healthy; help fight disease |
| Minerals | Kapenta (calcium), beans and dark green leaves (iron), milk (calcium), iodised salt (iodine) | Build bones and teeth, make blood, keep the body working well |
| Roughage (dietary fibre) | Roller meal, vegetables, beans, fruit and sweet potato skins | Helps food move through the gut; prevents constipation |
| Water | Drinking water, fruits like watermelon, soups, tea | Dissolves and carries substances, controls body temperature |

### More about each class

**Carbohydrates.** These are sugars and starch. Starch is found in nshima, cassava and potatoes. Sugar is found in sugar cane, honey and ripe fruit. They are the body's main source of energy.

**Proteins.** Proteins build new cells. They are needed most by growing children and by people healing from injury. They also repair worn-out tissues such as skin.

**Lipids.** Lipids are fats (solid at room temperature, like butter) and oils (liquid, like cooking oil). They give more than twice as much energy as the same mass of carbohydrate. Fat under the skin keeps the body warm. Fat around the kidneys and heart protects them.

**Vitamins.** Vitamins are needed in very small amounts. Vitamin A (mangoes, carrots, pumpkin) helps you see in dim light. Vitamin C (oranges, guavas) keeps gums and skin healthy. Vitamin D helps build strong bones; the skin makes it in sunlight.

**Minerals.** Minerals are also needed in small amounts. Calcium builds bones and teeth. Iron is needed to make red blood cells. Iodine is needed by the thyroid gland, which is why salt in Zambia is iodised.

**Roughage.** Roughage is the part of plant food that the body cannot digest. It is not absorbed, but it gives bulk to food. This helps the muscles of the gut push food along and prevents constipation.

**Water.** About two-thirds of your body is water. Water dissolves food so it can be absorbed, carries substances in the blood, and cools you through sweat.

### Why we need nutrients

The syllabus groups the uses of nutrients into four jobs:

| Job | Main nutrient classes |
| --- | --- |
| Energy supply | Carbohydrates, lipids |
| Growth | Proteins, minerals (e.g. calcium) |
| Tissue repair | Proteins |
| Protection (keeping healthy) | Vitamins, minerals, lipids (protect organs) |

Roughage and water keep the digestive system and all body processes working.

A diet that lacks one class of nutrient can cause a **deficiency disease**. You will study these in topic 1.3.4.

### Quick check

1. What is the main nutrient in cooking oil?
2. Which class of nutrient prevents constipation?
3. Beans contain protein and roughage. Give one job of each.

**Answers:** (1) Lipid. (2) Roughage. (3) Protein: growth and repair of tissues. Roughage: helps food move through the gut.

### Worked example 1: Classify a meal

Mwila eats nshima, kapenta fried in oil, and rape. Classify each food by its main nutrient.

- Nshima: carbohydrate (starch)
- Kapenta: protein (also gives calcium and some fat)
- Cooking oil: lipid
- Rape: vitamins and minerals (also roughage)

**Answer:** The meal contains carbohydrate, protein, lipid, vitamins, minerals and roughage. A glass of water would complete all seven classes.

### Worked example 2: One food, many nutrients

Groundnuts contain about 49 g fat, 26 g protein and 16 g carbohydrate in every 100 g.

**Question:** What is the main nutrient in groundnuts?

**Answer:** Lipid, because fat is the largest amount. Groundnuts are also a good source of protein. When you classify, always say the main nutrient first, then any other important ones.

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Nutrient | A substance in food that the body uses for energy, growth, repair or health |
| Classify | Put things into groups by what they have in common |
| Carbohydrate | Sugars and starch; the main energy food |
| Protein | Nutrient used for growth and repair |
| Lipid | Fats and oils; store energy and keep the body warm |
| Vitamin | Nutrient needed in tiny amounts to keep the body healthy |
| Mineral | Element from food, such as calcium or iron, needed in small amounts |
| Roughage | Plant material the body cannot digest; prevents constipation |
| Main nutrient | The nutrient a food contains the most of |
| Deficiency disease | Illness caused by lack of a nutrient in the diet |

### Summary

- A nutrient is a substance in food that the body needs.
- There are seven classes: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, vitamins, minerals, roughage and water.
- Most foods contain several nutrients; classify a food by its main one.

- Eating foods from all seven classes keeps the body healthy.

## 2. Practical: Sorting foods and the paper test for fats

**Competence ID:** BIO-F1-1.3.1.1 **Competence:** Classify types of food nutrients **Form:** 1 **Covers activities:** exploring sources of food nutrients; classifying foods by nutrient content

### Aim

1. To classify common Zambian foods by their main nutrient.
2. To find out which foods contain lipids, using the paper (grease-spot) test.

### Key term

**Grease spot:** a see-through patch left on paper by fat or oil. Water also wets paper, but it dries and disappears. A fat spot stays.

### Materials (no lab needed)

- Plain brown paper (a cut-up paper bag or sugar packet) or white exercise-book paper
- Small samples, about one teaspoon each: groundnuts, cooking oil, a piece of bread, a few grains of sugar, a slice of boiled cassava or sweet potato, a piece of avocado, kapenta (dried)
- A few drops of clean water
- A clean spoon or small stone for crushing
- A pencil and a ruler
- Empty food packets and labels from home (mealie meal, cooking oil, milk, biscuits)
- Seven pieces of paper or a chalkboard marked with the seven nutrient classes

### Safety

- Do not eat or taste any food used in the practical.
- Tell your teacher if you are allergic to groundnuts. Someone else can handle them.
- Wash your hands before and after.
- Wipe up spilt oil at once so no one slips.

### Method

**Part A: Sort the foods**

1. Lay out the seven class labels on a desk or the floor.
2. Put each food sample and each food packet on the label of its main nutrient.
3. Read the nutrition table on each packet. Check that the largest amount matches the class you chose. Move it if not.
4. Record your groups in a table.

**Part B: Paper test for fats**

1. Draw a pencil grid of squares on the paper. Write the name of one food in each square.
2. Rub or crush a small amount of each food onto its square. For cooking oil, use one drop.
3. In one square, put one drop of water. This is the **control**: it shows what a spot with no fat looks like.
4. Leave the paper to dry for 10 minutes, in sunlight if possible.
5. Hold the paper up to the light. Look for see-through patches.
6. Record "grease spot" or "no grease spot" for each food.

### Expected results

| Food | Grease spot after drying? | Main nutrient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water (control) | No | Water |
| Cooking oil | Yes, large | Lipid |
| Groundnuts | Yes | Lipid (also protein) |
| Avocado | Yes | Lipid |
| Kapenta | Faint | Protein (some lipid) |
| Bread | No (or very faint) | Carbohydrate |
| Boiled cassava | No | Carbohydrate |
| Sugar | No | Carbohydrate |

### What the results show

Foods that leave a grease spot contain lipids. The water spot dried away, so the see-through patches were caused by fat, not by wetness. Foods with no spot have little or no fat; they are mainly carbohydrate.

The paper test only shows lipids. Tests for starch, sugar and protein use chemicals. You will do those in Form 3.

### Questions

1. Why did we put a drop of water on the paper?
2. Which foods contained lipids?
3. Kapenta left only a faint spot. Does that mean kapenta has no nutrients? Explain.
4. Groundnuts belong in two classes. Name them.
5. Suggest one way to make this test fairer.

### Answers

1. As a control. It shows that a wet spot dries away, so any spot left is due to fat.
2. Cooking oil, groundnuts, avocado (and a little in kapenta).
3. No. Kapenta is mainly protein, which this test does not detect.
4. Lipids and proteins.
5. Use the same amount of each food; rub each for the same time; use the same type of paper; let all spots dry for the same time.

- A. Proteins
- B. Carbohydrates
- C. Vitamins
- D. Minerals

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Carbohydrates (starch and sugars) are broken down first to release energy; vitamins and minerals give no energy.

- B. Cooking oil

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Kapenta is mainly protein; cassava and sugar cane are mainly carbohydrate, and cooking oil is lipid.

### Q08

**ID:** BIO-F1-1.3.1.1-Q08 **Type:** Short answer · **AO:** B · **Marks:** 3 · **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** Chanda eats nshima made from breakfast mealie meal with beef every day. He rarely eats vegetables or fruit. He often has constipation. Explain why Chanda gets constipated, and suggest one change to his diet.

**Correct answer:** His diet lacks roughage, so food moves slowly through his gut. He should eat more vegetables, fruit or beans, or use roller meal.

**Explanation:** Breakfast meal and beef contain very little roughage.

**Marking points:**

- Diet has little roughage / fibre (1)
- Roughage gives bulk that helps the gut push food along / without it food moves slowly (1)
- Any one suitable change: more vegetables, fruit or beans; roller meal instead of breakfast meal (1)

## 4. Video script: "What's on your plate?"

**Competence ID:** BIO-F1-1.3.1.1 **Competence:** Classify types of food nutrients **Form:** 1 **Length:** about 4.5 minutes (about 640 spoken words) **Setting:** a presenter at a market stall, then at a kitchen table

### Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:30)

*Visual: presenter at a busy market. Tomatoes, kapenta, mealie meal, mangoes on the stall.*

**PRESENTER:** Hello everyone! Look at all this food. Kapenta, mealie meal, mangoes, groundnuts. Your body needs all of it, but not for the same reasons. Today we'll sort food into seven groups called nutrient classes. By the end, you'll be able to look at any plate and say what it's doing for your body.

*On screen: "BIO-F1-1.3.1.1 Classify types of food nutrients"*

### Scene 2 — What is a nutrient? (0:30–1:00)

*Visual: presenter holds up a packet of kapenta.*

**PRESENTER:** First, a key word. A nutrient is a substance in food that your body uses to live, grow and stay healthy.

Here's the thing: most foods have more than one nutrient. Kapenta has protein, some fat and calcium. So we sort food by its main nutrient: the one it has the most of.

*On screen: "Nutrient = a substance in food the body needs"*

### Scene 3 — The seven classes (1:00–3:15)

*Visual: kitchen table. Presenter places each food on a coloured card as it is named.*

**PRESENTER:** Let's meet the seven classes.

Number one: **carbohydrates**. That's sugars and starch. Nshima, cassava, rice, sweet potatoes. Their job? Energy. They're the fuel that gets you to school and through football practice.

Number two: **proteins**. Beans, kapenta, eggs, chicken, edible caterpillars. Proteins build your body. They're for growth, and for repair. Cut your knee? Protein helps fix it.

Number three: **lipids**. That's fats and oils. Cooking oil, butter, groundnuts, avocado. Lipids store energy, about twice as much as carbohydrates. A layer of fat under the skin keeps you warm, and fat around your heart and kidneys protects them.

Number four: **vitamins**. Mangoes, oranges, guavas, pumpkin leaves (chibwabwa), rape. You only need tiny amounts, but they keep you healthy. Vitamin A helps you see in dim light. Vitamin C keeps your gums healthy.

Number five: **minerals**. Also needed in small amounts. Calcium from milk and kapenta builds bones and teeth. Iron from beans and dark green leaves helps make blood. And iodine? It's added to our salt. Check the packet: it says "iodised".

Number six: **roughage**, also called fibre. Roller meal, vegetables, fruit skins. Your body can't digest roughage. But it gives food bulk, so your gut can push it along. No roughage, and you get constipated.

And number seven: **water**. About two-thirds of you is water! It dissolves food, carries things in your blood, and cools you down when you sweat.

*On screen: all seven cards in a row: Carbohydrates, Proteins, Lipids, Vitamins, Minerals, Roughage, Water.*

### Scene 4 — Try it yourself (3:15–4:00)

*Visual: a plate of nshima, kapenta with tomato, and rape.*

**PRESENTER:** Your turn. Pause the video and classify this plate.

*On screen: "PAUSE — classify each food" (3 seconds)*

**PRESENTER:** Ready? Nshima: carbohydrate. Kapenta: protein. The oil it's fried in: lipid. Rape and tomato: vitamins, minerals, and roughage too. Add a glass of water, and this plate covers all seven classes. Well done if you got them!

### Scene 5 — Summary (4:00–4:30)

*Visual: presenter back at the market.*

**PRESENTER:** So, remember: seven classes. Carbohydrates and lipids for energy. Proteins for growth and repair. Vitamins and minerals to keep you healthy. Roughage to keep food moving. Water to dissolve and carry.

Next time you eat, look at your plate and ask: which classes are here, and which are missing? See you in the next lesson!

*On screen: "Next: Dietary needs of different persons (1.3.2)"*

- Keep each on-screen caption under 8 words so it reads on a small phone.
